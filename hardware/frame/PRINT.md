# Printing and assembly (PETG, Anycubic Kobra 2 Neo)

Plate v2: motors 180 × 167 mm (wheelbase 245), prop gap 37.5 mm along Y and 50 mm along X.
No longitudinal beams: arm stiffness comes from **two 3 × 6 mm side ribs along the arm edges** (U-section, I ≈ 1330 mm⁴, same as the old 6 × 6 rib; arm deflection at 16 N ≈ 1.6 mm, resonance ≈ 75 Hz).
Between the side ribs a 9.5–12.5 mm channel for the motor wires (2026-09-05: the center rib was in the way of the wires). Side ribs are interrupted around the leg nylocs (mount A) and the standoffs (B).
Beams would only help in pitch (in roll both motors on a side move together), so removed: −26 g.

Files: `print/*.stl` (already in print orientation, generated by `export_print.py`).

PETG settings: nozzle 235–245 °C, bed 75–80 °C, fan 30–50 %, speed ≤ 80 mm/s on the plate and legs, layer 0.2, first layer 0.24.
Holes are modeled with +0.25–0.4 mm compensation; if M3 does not fit, drill out to 3.2 mm.
M4 nut pockets in the GPS mast flange: 3.8 mm tall (the pocket roof is a bridge and sags). If a nut does not go in, a few strokes with a flat needle file, do not force it.
Standoffs have no nuts: the M4x8 forms its own thread in the ø3.5 pilot. Drive it in slowly the first time; if a thread ever strips, drill 4.5 mm and switch to the nut version (`STANDOFF_NUTS = True` in accessories.py).

## Assembly order
1. **Motors** on the plate pads from above, screws from below. Pattern: 4 holes in a cross on a ø16 circle (opposite holes 16 mm apart). Check against the motor before printing the whole plate (see test below). Turn the motor so the wires exit along the arm; route the wires in the channel between the side ribs (zip ties through the channel) toward the center.
2. **Stack** on the plate, 30.5 pattern, ESC below, FC on top, stack USB-C sideways toward any window. Capacitor next to the ESC, between the stack and the arm.
3. **Standoffs**: no nuts. Run an M4x8 into each end once by hand to cut the thread (the ø3.5 pilot prints horizontally and is slightly oval, which helps the thread bite), then back it out.
4. **Legs**: oval leg flange under the plate on the arm widening (mount A, 40 mm from the motor), blade outward toward the motor; two bolts either side of the arm axis (±8 mm), nylocs on top in the side rib gaps.
   Two M3×14 bolts from below, nyloc nuts on top, plus a 2.5–3.6 mm zip tie around the arm and flange along the hole line (side ribs are interrupted there, the tie lies on the plate).
   NOTE: v2 legs do not fit the old printed plate (motors 90 × 70): the A holes are elsewhere. Deck and standoffs fit (B holes are the same).
5. **Standoffs** on the four B holes (±34, ±18.8): M4×8 bolt from under the plate through the ø8×2.5 counterbore into the standoff, 4.5 mm of thread in the plastic.
   On the old printed plate the B holes are 3.2 mm: drill to 4.3 and counterbore from below with an 8 mm drill, 2.5 mm deep.
   Standoffs deliberately not on A: A holes are 40 mm from the motor, inside the prop disc; B are at 76 mm, outside.
6. **Deck** onto the standoffs, 4 × M4×8 from above straight into the standoffs (5 mm of thread). Pi 5 on 6–8 mm standoffs, Pi USB/Ethernet facing back (−X).
7. **GPS mast** on the deck at the rear: 4 M4 nuts into the side pockets of the mast flange, 4 × M4×8 from below through the deck into those nuts.
   The GPS module itself (round puck ø50) **is not printed**, it sits in the mast cup: 1 mm double-sided tape on the cup floor, 2.5 mm rim keeps it from sliding,
   two zip ties crosswise through the 4 slots in the cup. Cable through the notch in the rim, down the tube, to FC UART6 + SDA/SCL.
   If the puck is not ø50, change `GPS.puck_d` in params.py and regenerate.
8. **Camera cradle** (replaceable): set it on the plate front, flange over the holes at (44, ±9); 2 × M4×8 from below through the plate counterbores, plain M4 nuts on top of the flange (7 mm socket, the cassette sits 6 mm further forward so the socket fits). The bolt engages 2.5 mm of the nut, enough for a 12 g camera. A pan head (DIN 7985, ø8 × 3.1) sticks 0.6 mm out of the counterbore, that is fine, the battery is 2 mm lower; if the head does not go into the ø8 counterbore, run an 8.5 drill through.
   The camera board (25 × 24) drops in from the top into the slot along the edges, lens forward, ribbon up, and rests on the rear wall. Fastening: **4 M2 × 5–6 mm screws** (machine or self-tapping for plastic) from the front through the board holes into the ø1.7 pilot holes of the bosses on the rear wall; the screw cuts its own thread, depth 4.6 mm. Boss pattern 21 × 12.5 (Pi Camera v2); if the board does not fit, the bosses are ø6 and can be redrilled ±0.7 mm. Two screws on a diagonal are enough.
   Different camera = new cradle, the plate does not change. The 15° downward tilt is set by `CAM_CRADLE["tilt_deg"]`.
9. **Battery** under the plate along X, on the anti-slip strip. Two 4.8 mm zip ties: down through slot (−25, +23.5), under the battery, up through (−25, −23.5), lock on top; the second through the slots at x = +25. Across the top of the plate the tie runs under the ESC (stack must sit on standoffs/dampers ≥ 4 mm) between the stack and the deck standoffs. Tighten the ties after pressing the battery onto the tape. XT60 to power board to ESC.
   Shift the battery center **≈ 7 mm forward** (toward the camera) to compensate for the GPS mast and Pi at the rear; CG estimate: mass ≈ 1.2 kg, without the shift CG is 3.8 mm behind the motor center.
10. **BEC 5 V / 5 A (Mini560 Pro)** is required: Pi 5 wants up to 5 A, the FC BEC gives 2 A. Mounting: module in heat shrink, under the deck between the cross members, zip tie through the ø3.4 holes on the cross member (±34, ±9). Input from the XT60 power board, output to the Pi USB-C (cable with a cut end) or the 5V/GND GPIO pins. Wires to the Pi no longer than 15 cm, cross section ≥ 0.5 mm².

## Test before printing the plate
Print `print/test_motor_pad.stl` (motor pad, 10 minutes): the motor must sit on 4 screws without strain. If it does not, measure the distance between opposite motor holes with calipers and correct `MOTOR_HOLE_R` in plate_v2.py.

## Checks before first flight
- Balance: the drone resting on its center box on the edge of a ruler across X must not tip; otherwise move the battery.
- Ground clearance from ground to bottom of the battery ≥ 30 mm with the drone standing.
- Props do not touch the deck or legs (prop discs at ≈ 28 mm, deck at 41, legs under the plate).
- Stack does not touch the plate ribs; USB-C accessible.
- Compass on the GPS puck ≥ 40 mm above the power wires; calibrate the compass in ArduPilot after assembly.
